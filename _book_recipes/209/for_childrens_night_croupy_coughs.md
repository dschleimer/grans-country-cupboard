# for Childrens Night Croupy Coughs

<div style="background-color: #fee; border: 3px solid #c00; border-radius: 8px; padding: 1em 1.2em; margin: 1em 0;">
<strong style="color: #c00; font-size: 1.1em;">&#9888; Do Not Follow This Remedy</strong><br>
This historical home remedy is included only for completeness. <strong>Do not use this for croup or coughing in children.</strong> Croup can be serious — if your child has a barking cough or difficulty breathing, please consult a doctor or see <a href="https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/croup/">NHS — Croup</a> for current medical guidance.
</div>

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount  | Notes |
| ---------- | ------- | ----- |
| Molasses   | 1 Tbsp  | |
| Lard       | 1 Tbsp  | |
| Alum       | 1 Pinch | |

## Method

1. Keep a small pan in a warm place such as on a radiator in the bedroom
2. Mix molasses, lard, and a pinch of alum together in the pan
3. Dose — 1 tsp to stop coughing

## Modern Notes

* From 1903, Mother Marter — a historical home remedy, **not modern medical advice**
* Alum (potassium aluminum sulfate) was commonly used in folk medicine as an astringent
* This type of syrup-and-fat remedy was a standard approach to childhood croup before modern medicine
